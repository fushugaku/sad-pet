# 303 Bass

A monophonic source module in SAD PET's Rack. Choose **303 Bass** in the
Timbres menu to create a Rack with that source, or add **303 Bass** through
**+ Module** in any Rack. It can be moved, powered off and sent through its
Mixer strip, filters, grains and effects. Earlier standalone 303 projects
migrate to the Rack structure while retaining their pattern and controls.
Use the scene's Play button. Its pattern has 1–16
sixteenth-note steps, each with pitch, note/rest, accent and a slide into the
following step. Turning Pattern off leaves the voice available to MIDI.
Overlapping MIDI notes slide with last-note priority; velocities of 100–127
select accent. Pitch bend spans two semitones. All voice controls and
all pattern cells have stable, appended host parameter IDs. Scenes and
instrument presets carry the complete settings.

**+ Oscillator** in the RADIAS section adds up to four layers from the shared
Rustias OSC1 implementation: saw, pulse, triangle, sine, formant and noise;
Wave, Cross, Unison and VPM modes; individual pitch, fine tuning and level;
and Ring/Sync links to the original **303** oscillator or another RADIAS layer.
The original VCO is also a Cross source. Hard sync uses its actual phase wraps,
and modulation uses its unmixed wave even with OSC LEVEL at zero. The layers follow the 303's note and slide,
and sum with its original oscillator before the same mono ladder and VCA.
OSC LEVEL and OSC PITCH change only the original 303 oscillator. With no added
layers and these controls at their defaults, the original circuit model is
unchanged. The added generators are a software extension, not original hardware.

Neuropolis **303 pattern pitch** (Main pitch) transposes the pattern's notes and all its RADIAS layers.
**303 RADIAS OSC N pitch offset** adds an independent offset and optional gate
for a layer. Parameter lanes and step destinations can change the 303 cutoff,
resonance, envelope, accent, oscillator pitch/level, and RADIAS controls.
The 303 sequencer stays visible and keeps its own notes, rests, accents and
slides while Neuropolis transposes and modulates it. Notes lanes do not
replace an enabled 303 pattern. To play direct notes instead, explicitly turn
**Pattern** off: then **Notes → 303 + RADIAS** controls notes/gates, tied notes
slide and velocity 100 or above selects accent. Turning Pattern on restores
its own sequence. All these routes survive scene and instrument-preset recall.

With a 303 module in the Rack, **303** appears alongside ENV 1–3 in every
external filter's envelope selector, including Filter 1/2 and added filters.
It reads the voice's actual main envelope, with its decay, shorter accented
decay and legato behavior; each filter applies its own ENV DEPTH. A Rack with
no 303 supplies zero for a stored 303 source instead of substituting another
envelope.

## Mods

The extra controls follow the functions described in the
[TD-3-MO quick-start guide](https://mediadl.musictribe.com/media/PLM/data/docs/P0EYF/QSG_BE_0718-ACE_TD-3-MO_WW.pdf).
They remain part of the same Rack source and are available to Neuropolis,
modulation and host automation.

| Control | SAD PET behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Soft Attack | Normal-note VCA attack, 0.3–30 ms; accented attacks stay sharp. |
| Decay Normal | The existing normal filter-envelope decay, 200–2500 ms. |
| Decay Accent | Independent accented filter-envelope decay, approximately 30–3000 ms. |
| Filter Track | 0–200% pitch tracking around C2; 100% follows one octave per octave. |
| Slide Time | Original RC time through five times longer; display shows approximately 95% settling time. |
| Sweep Speed | Fast pulse, original Normal capacitor memory, or slower, wider sweep. |
| Accent Sweep | Normal; Hi Res with twice the filter feedback; or sweep off with Hi Res. |
| Filter FM | AC-coupled VCA output modulates filter frequency at the oversampled audio rate. |

The [original Devil Fish manual](https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/dfish/Devil-Fish-Manual.pdf)
provides useful attack, slide and accent-circuit references. These time ranges,
tracking scale and FM depth are software calibrations, not measurements of a
TD-3-MO. Fast/Slow use reduced charge models; FM uses one internal-sample delay
and a nominal 20 Hz coupling pole. Normal settings retain the existing model,
and new host controls append without moving any previous parameter index.

## Circuit basis

The [Roland service notes, main board on page 5](https://www.synfo.nl/servicemanuals/Roland/ROLAND_TB-303_SERVICE_NOTES.pdf)
provide the oscillator/control topology and component values. The ladder uses
C18 = 18 nF and C19/C24/C26 = 33 nF. Slide follows R91 = 100 kΩ and C35 = 220 nF:
a 22 ms time constant, approximately 66 ms to cover 95% of an interval. Tuning
spans ±7 semitones. The main envelope's measured 100%-to-10% decay range in the
service drawing is 200 ms to 2.5 s. The oscillator is free-running: saw and
square share one phase, which note triggers never reset.

The filter implementation follows the loaded diode topology analyzed by
[Tim Stinchcombe](https://www.timstinchcombe.co.uk/index.php?pge=diode), including
the fact that adjacent stages are not buffered. With normalized differential
capacitor voltages `x`, currents `j0=tanh(x0-x1)`, `j1=tanh(x1-x2)`,
`j2=tanh(x2-x3)`, `j3=tanh(x3)`, and input-pair current `ji=tanh(input-feedback)`,
the derivatives are proportional to:

```
(C/C18) * (ji - j0)
j0 - j1
j1 - j2
j2 - j3
```

The small-signal core denominator, using `p=s/g` and `r=C/C18`, is
`p⁴ + (6+r)p³ + (10+5r)p² + (4+6r)p + r`; its numerator is `r`.
This is tested against the independently evaluated frequency response.
The [extended coupling-network analysis](https://www.timstinchcombe.co.uk/index.php?pge=diode2)
supplies six additional poles and their forward/feedback zeros, retained as
bilinear first-order sections. The feedback sections expose their instantaneous
affine output to the same implicit solve as the nonlinear ladder. There is no
extra one-sample delay in the resonance loop. Trapezoidal integration and
bounded Newton iterations update the four capacitor states together.

[Robin Whittle's accent investigation](https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/dfish/303-unique.html)
describes two envelopes, the short main-envelope decay on accented notes, and
the additional accent paths to VCA and filter. The model retains charge in the
47 kΩ / 100 kΩ resonance-pot / 1 µF sweep network, with its 100 kΩ load and a
piecewise diode, and smooths the VCA accent through 47 kΩ / 33 nF. Consecutive
accents therefore interact with the existing charge. Env Mod retains a nonzero
minimum. [Slide/gate observations](https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/dfish/303-slide.html)
inform the pattern's tied gates and slide-on-arrival behavior; these notes also
flag uncertainty about some original sequencer timing. The pattern uses a
7/12-step ordinary gate. [Whittle's manual](https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/dfish/Devil-Fish-Manual.pdf)
reports the stock VCA's long decay and delayed attack; the model uses a 3.5 s
100%-to-10% decay and a nominal 4 ms delay plus 3 ms attack.

## Fidelity boundaries

This is a reduced circuit-derived DSP model, not a transistor-level SPICE
simulation of the complete board or an emulation of the original CPU ROM.
The VCO uses analytical band-limited ramps and comparator-like square waves;
its transistor reset transient, temperature drift and individual component
mismatch are not modeled. The OTA/VCA uses a smooth differential saturation
approximation. Coupling sections reproduce the linearized transfer network;
putting that equivalent network around the nonlinear core does not reproduce
every nonlinear interaction of the original amplifiers. Cutoff, envelope
current scaling and output gain are nominal calibrations. Hardware recordings
or measurements are still needed to assess and tune exact unit-to-unit fidelity.

The engine oversamples to at least 176.4 kHz, then uses cascaded anti-alias
filters to return to the host rate. Controls slew over 2 ms. Oscillator, filter,
envelopes, accent charge, pattern position and held notes survive ordinary
preset edits. Runtime allocation and retirement take place off the native audio
callback. The complete voice feeds the existing effect chain and Bus sends;
Master protection applies after the scene sum.

## Verification

Rust tests check the small-signal circuit response, RC slide across sample rates,
non-retriggered legato, repeated-accent charge, shortened accent decay, parameter
extremes and Stop. The compiled-WASM test exercises the sixteen steps, MIDI,
scene recall, block-size independence and Bus routing. The native integration
test checks rendering and preset/parameter updates without audio-thread heap
operations. These are model and integration checks, not a claim of a measured
match to original hardware.
